## Theme

PBS / Decomposition — Document FBS-PBS-WBS relationships per INCOSE §2.3.4.1

## Evidence

- `system_definition/fbs/00-intent/use-cases.md` — Documents functional use cases (UC-001 through UC-014). These represent the functional architecture but are not explicitly identified as FBS elements.
- `system_definition/fbs/01-product/product-commitments.md` — Documents product commitments (PC-001 through PC-010). These bridge functional intent and physical architecture but do not declare their decomposition role.
- `system_definition/pbs/02-architecture/architecture.md` — Contains control-flow diagrams, stable concepts, and boundaries representing physical architecture. No explicit boundary between functional and physical decomposition.
- `SE_V5/INCOSE_SEHB5.txt` lines 4767–4776 (§2.3.4.1) — States: "The SE practice is to derive system functions from requirements and then allocate these functions into products or services, usually through the development of a functional and physical architecture." Describes FBS as "a breakdown of the functions of the required SoI into successively lower levels of its functional architecture" and PBS as a breakdown of physical architecture. States WBS "is based on the FBS in the initial stages of system maturity and the PBS in the later stages."

## Current Pain Or Risk

- Current PBS mixes functional concerns (intent, product commitments, use cases) and physical concerns (architecture, implementation) without stating where the FBS ends and the PBS begins.
- New contributors may not understand that use cases (UC-*) and commitments (PC-*) belong to the functional breakdown, while agents and prompts belong to the physical breakdown.
- No WBS mapping exists, making it unclear which work activities (planning, building, reviewing, reflecting, reporting) correspond to which FBS or PBS elements.
- The transition from FBS-based WBS (early design) to PBS-based WBS (later stages) is invisible.

## Proposed Improvement

Add `system_definition/breakdown-structures.md` that explicitly documents:
1. **FBS (Functional Breakdown Structure)**: Map use cases (UC-001 through UC-014) and product commitments (PC-001 through PC-010) as the functional architecture tree.
2. **PBS (Physical Breakdown Structure)**: Map the agent hierarchy (orchestrator, planner, builder, reviewer, reflection, reporter) and artifact tree as the physical architecture.
3. **WBS (Work Breakdown Structure)**: Map the workflow stages (planning, implementation, review, reflection, reporting) as activities derived from FBS in early stages and PBS in later stages.
4. **Boundary**: Explicitly state which artifacts belong to FBS vs. PBS and how the boundary is maintained.

## Expected Benefit

- Clarifies which parts of the PBS are functional vs. physical per INCOSE §2.3.4.1.
- Enables traceability from requirements (FBS) through design (PBS) to work activities (WBS).
- Helps new contributors understand the decomposition model.
- Provides a reference for WBS planning in future implementation projects.

## Risk And Blast Radius

**Low** — documentation clarification only. Risks:
- The FBS/PBS boundary may be contested; needs maintainer agreement on the dividing line.
- Must stay synchronized with changes to use cases, commitments, architecture, and implementation.
- WBS mapping is informational and should not be treated as a project management artifact.
